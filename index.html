Our research

How do host–microbe interactions shape immunity and cancer?

Our laboratory studies how microbial signals and antigen-specific immune responses interact to regulate tissue homeostasis, inflammation, and tumor immunity. We combine mouse genetics, immunology, microbiology, single-cell approaches, and translational studies to uncover mechanisms that connect the microbiome with systemic immune responses.

Microbial metabolites and cancer

Understanding how metabolites produced or modified by intestinal microbes influence tumor growth and anti-tumor immunity.

RORγt⁺ immune cells

Defining the interactions among RORγt⁺ antigen- presenting cells, regulatory T cells, and other immune populations in health and disease.

Antigen-specific immunity

Investigating how microbial antigens shape antigen-specific T-cell responses and immune tolerance.

Human cancer and translational immunology

Extending mechanistic discoveries from experimental models to human cancers and therapeutic settings.
