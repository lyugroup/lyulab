Research

Host–Microbe Interactions

The Lyu lab investigates how gut microbes and their products communicate with the host to regulate inflammation, cancer development and progression, and responses to cancer immunotherapy. We use multiomic approaches, together with studies of microbial signals and antigen-specific immune responses, to define mechanisms linking host–microbe interactions to disease. Our long-term goal is to uncover new mechanisms and develop therapeutic strategies for cancer and inflammatory diseases.

01

MICROBIAL METABOLITES AND CANCER

Identifying novel microbial products and defining how they influence cancer development, progression, and responses to cancer immunotherapy.

02

ANTIGEN-SPECIFIC IMMUNE RESPONSES AND CANCER

Investigating how microbial antigens and antigen-specific immune responses shape inflammation, immune regulation, and cancer progression.

03

TRANSLATIONAL STUDIES IN HUMAN CANCER

Translating insights from experimental models to human cancer to identify host–microbe interactions and immune pathways with potential for therapeutic application.

Meet the Lyu Lab

Mengze Lyu, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor
Division of Medical Oncology
Departments of Medicine and Biochemistry & Molecular Biology
Robert Wood Johnson Medical School
Rutgers University

Dr. Mengze Lyu earned his Ph.D. in Cell Biology at Peking University, where he identified manganese as a key regulator of antitumor immunity through activation of the cGAS-STING pathway. He subsequently pursued postdoctoral training with Dr. Gregory F. Sonnenberg at Weill Cornell Medicine, where his research focused on the complex interplay between host immunity and the gut microbiota.


His work has defined the heterogeneity and immunological functions of RORγt⁺ antigen-presenting cells and their roles in immune tolerance and inflammation. Dr. Lyu's long-term goal is to uncover fundamental mechanisms of host–microbe crosstalk in chronic inflammation and cancer, with the ultimate aim of developing novel strategies for cancer and inflammatory disease treatment.
